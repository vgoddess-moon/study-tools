Chapter 18: Concepts of Care for Patients with Cancer

+

Carcinogenesis & Cancer Development 4 stages

+
Professor Emphasis
Four Stages of Carcinogenesis
  • Initiation — Irreversible DNA damage by a carcinogen. "Make sure it's irreversible."
  • Promotion — Hormones (estrogen) or insulin fuel growth of initiated cells.
  • Progression — Tumor develops its own blood supply (vascularization), becomes more malignant.
  • Metastasis — Cells break off, enter blood/lymph, establish distant colonies.
Fast Rule
If you see [initiation] → think [irreversible DNA damage]. If you see [hormones fueling cancer] → think [Promotion stage].
Professor Emphasis
Primary vs Secondary Tumors
Secondary tumors are always the same cancer type as the primary parent tissue. "You need to know where the parent cell is to know how to treat it." Not all cancers are treated equally.
Exam Trap
Students confuse initiation with promotion. Initiation = permanent DNA change. Promotion = fuel/growth enhancement. They are NOT the same thing.

Classification & Staging

+
TNM Classification
T = Tumor size · N = Node involvement · M = Metastasis (distant spread)
Grading (G1–G4)
G1 = well-differentiated (looks like parent) → G4 = anaplastic (no resemblance to normal tissue, most aggressive)
Ploidy
Chromosome number. Normal = euploid/diploid. Cancer = aneuploid (abnormal chromosome count).
Professor Emphasis
Know These Systems
"Please read over those, know what they are, know how cancer is graded, know how cancer has their staging."

Prevention & Screening

+
Professor Emphasis
Primary vs Secondary Prevention
"You have to know what's in the primary and what's in the secondary... You guys need to remember those."
  • Primary (stop BEFORE it starts): Avoid carcinogens, modify risks, remove at-risk tissue (prophylactic mastectomy), chemoprevention, vaccination (HPV)
  • Secondary (catch early/screening): Mammograms, fecal occult blood, colonoscopy, cervical screenings
Fast Rule
If you see [vaccine / avoid carcinogens / tissue removal] → Primary Prevention. If you see [screening test / mammogram / colonoscopy] → Secondary Prevention.
Exam Trap
Genetic testing (BRCA1/2) provides RISK information only — it does NOT diagnose cancer and does NOT guarantee you will get it.

Treatment Types

+
Professor Emphasis
Surgery Categories
  • Prophylactic — Removing precancerous tissue
  • Diagnostic — Biopsy/testing
  • Curative — Complete tumor removal
  • Debulking — Decrease size, NOT curative
  • Palliative — Comfort only, not to cure
Professor Emphasis
Neoadjuvant vs Adjuvant
"Please know the difference between the one that has the neo and the one that doesn't."
  • Neoadjuvant = BEFORE surgery (shrink tumor)
  • Adjuvant = AFTER surgery (kill remaining cells)
Professor Emphasis
Radiation: External vs Brachytherapy
Brachytherapy = internal radioactive seeds. "You are radioactive while you have this... you don't want to be in the same bed with your family." External beam does NOT make the patient radioactive.
Professor Emphasis
Extravasation
"Okay guys, you need to remember these things." Extravasation = dangerous leakage of IV vesicant drugs into subcutaneous tissue → severe tissue damage and loss. STOP infusion immediately.
Fast Rule
Oral chemo is just as toxic as IV chemo — to the patient AND the handler. Requires PPE, cannot flush down toilet.

Side Effects & Nursing Care

+
Professor Emphasis
Bone Marrow Suppression & Nadir
Chemo drops WBCs and platelets. Lowest point = Nadir (7-10 days post-chemo). Teach infection prevention (handwashing) and bleeding precautions (electric shavers).
Professor Emphasis
Mucositis
"What do you definitely don't want to give them?" — NO alcohol mouthwash, NO acidic items (burns and dries). Treat with ice chips and soft toothbrush.
Professor Emphasis
Alopecia
Hair grows back ~1 month after chemo but "may be different from the original color, texture, or thickness."
Chemo Brain
Memory loss, difficulty learning. Provide printouts, include family in teaching sessions.
CIPN (Chemotherapy-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy)
Loss of sensory/motor function in peripheral nerves. Needs OT, teach injury prevention.
Professor Emphasis
Colony-Stimulating Factors
"Most common side effect is what my big mouth just said — bone pain." CSFs (filgrastim) stimulate bone marrow recovery after myelosuppression.

Oncologic Emergencies 7 types

+
Professor Emphasis
DIC (Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation)
"Clotting and bleeding at the same time — wasting your resources." Body forms excessive micro-clots, depletes all clotting factors, then cannot stop bleeding.
SIADH
Tumor secretes its own ADH → kidneys retain too much water → dilutional hyponatremia. Weight gain, weakness, fluid restriction needed.
Spinal Cord Compression
Tumor enters spinal cord or collapses vertebra → neurologic damage → possible irreversible paralysis.
Hypercalcemia
Bone metastasis stimulates osteoclasts (bone breakdown) → calcium dumped into blood >10.5 mg/dL. Muscle weakness, EKG changes.
SVC Syndrome
Tumor/clot blocks venous return from head → facial edema, engorged blood vessels, stridor.
Professor Emphasis
Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS)
Chemo/radiation destroys bulky tumor cells rapidly → intracellular contents (K+, uric acid) dump into blood faster than kidneys can clear. Most commonly occurs DURING active treatment. Needs aggressive hydration.
Fast Rule
If you see [high K+ + high uric acid + during chemo] → think Tumor Lysis Syndrome. If you see [facial edema + distended neck veins] → think SVC Syndrome.

Key Medications

+
Antimetabolites
5-FU, capecitabine, cytarabine. Act as counterfeit metabolites fooling cancer cells.
Antitumor Antibiotics
Doxorubicin, bleomycin. Lifetime max dose due to cardiotoxicity.
Antimitotics
Paclitaxel, vincristine. Interfere with microtubules. High risk: peripheral neuropathy.
Alkylating Agents
Cyclophosphamide, cisplatin, carboplatin. Requires aggressive hydration.
Topoisomerase Inhibitors
Irinotecan, topotecan. Hallmark side effect: severe diarrhea.
Colony-Stimulating Factors
Filgrastim (Neupogen). Recovers bone marrow. Side effect: bone pain.
Serotonin Antagonists
Ondansetron (Zofran), granisetron. Anti-nausea for chemo patients.

Chapter 19: Concepts of Care for Patients with Infection

+

Infection Basics 6 terms

+
Professor Emphasis
"You Need to Know These Terms"
The professor explicitly stated these core definitions are testable.
Infection
Invasion of pathogens into the body that multiply and cause illness.
Virulence
How strong/dangerous a germ is — ability to cause severe disease.
Communicability
How easily a pathogen spreads from one person to another.
Microbiome
Genomes of all normal flora/microorganisms that naturally coexist in and on a person.
Endotoxins vs Exotoxins
Endotoxins = inside cell wall, released when bacteria break apart. Exotoxins = secreted outside by bacteria.
Fast Rule
Virulence = how DANGEROUS. Communicability = how CONTAGIOUS. They are independent.

Transmission Routes & Methods

+
Professor Emphasis
5 Routes of Transmission
  • Respiratory tract
  • GI tract
  • GU tract
  • Intact skin/mucous membranes
  • Bloodstream
Professor Emphasis
3 Methods of Transmission
  • Contact (direct and indirect)
  • Droplet (short distance, talking/sneezing)
  • Airborne (remains suspended in air)
Endemic / Epidemic / Pandemic
Endemic = predictable rate in a region (seasonal flu). Epidemic = more infections than expected. Pandemic = worldwide spread (COVID-19).
Professor Emphasis
Reservoirs
Hands, stethoscopes, and the wheels of computer “WOW” carts are major reservoirs for pathogens.

Infection Control & Hand Hygiene

+
Professor Emphasis
#1 Infection Control = HAND HYGIENE
Wash with soap and water for 15-30 seconds. The professor stressed this multiple times.
Exam Trap
ABHR does NOT kill C. difficile spores. Also NOT appropriate for: visibly soiled hands, after using the bathroom, or sticky hands. Students assume ABHR works for everything — it doesn't.
Professor Emphasis
Transmission-Based Precautions
"Please read in the book exactly what falls under standard, what falls under airborne, what falls under droplets. Know your precautions."
  • Airborne → N95/PAPR + negative pressure room (TB, measles, varicella)
  • Droplet → Surgical mask within 3-6 ft (flu, pertussis, meningitis)
  • Contact → Gown + gloves (MRSA, VRE, C. diff, scabies)
Cohorting
Grouping patients infected with the SAME pathogen in the same room.
Fast Rule
Visitor protection: Surgical mask protects the WEARER. Plus hand hygiene, gown/gloves, and do NOT visit if you have a cough or fever.

HAIs — Endogenous vs Exogenous

+
HAI (Health Care-Associated Infection)
Acquired in inpatient setting, NOT present or incubating at admission. Hospital “owns” it after 48-72 hours.
Endogenous Infection
Patient's own normal flora moves to the wrong place (E. coli from ruptured appendix into wound).
Exogenous Infection
From OUTSIDE the patient — HCW hands, contaminated tubes, implants.
Fast Rule
If you see [own flora in wrong place] → Endogenous. If you see [HCW hands / contaminated equipment] → Exogenous.

Antimicrobial Resistance & MDROs

+
Professor Emphasis
Causes of Antimicrobial Resistance
  • Wrong drug choice (Z-pack for a virus)
  • Inadequate dosing
  • Patient nonadherence (stopping pills early to “save them for next time”)
Conjugation
Surviving resistant bacteria physically touch other bacteria and transfer genetic material/resistance information. How bacteria “get smart.”
Professor Emphasis
HA-MRSA
Spread by direct contact (catheters, vascular access, wounds). Requires Contact isolation + private room + dedicated equipment. Susceptible to: IV vancomycin, oral linezolid, IV ceftaroline fosamil.
CA-MRSA
Affects healthy, non-hospitalized people. Teach: frequent hand hygiene, avoid crowds, good hygiene.
VRE
Vancomycin-Resistant Enterococcus. Lives on surfaces for days to weeks. Vancomycin will NOT work.
CRE
Carbapenem-Resistant Enterobacteriaceae (Klebsiella, E. coli). Resistant to penicillins and cephalosporins.
Professor Emphasis
C. difficile
Spread by direct contact. Causes severe diarrhea. ABHR does NOT kill spores — must use soap and water. When antibiotics fail, treat with FMT (Fecal Microbiota Transplantation) to restore normal flora.

Assessment, Labs & Fever Management

+
Professor Emphasis
Culture & Sensitivity
Identifies the organism AND which antibiotics will kill it. Results in 24-48 hours. Broad-spectrum is bridge until results return.
Professor Emphasis
ESR
"Know what it is and its significance." Elevated ESR = inflammation/infection present. It's a nonspecific marker — doesn't tell you WHAT, just that something is happening.
Professor Emphasis
Fever Management Priority
Priority = eliminate the underlying CAUSE with antimicrobials. Lowering temperature (antipyretics, cooling) is supportive but NOT the priority. Also replace fluids lost from perspiration/evaporation.
DOT (Directly Observed Therapy)
HCW must directly observe patient swallowing every dose. Used for TB to ensure adherence and prevent resistance.
Professor Emphasis
Septic Shock
Sepsis-induced distributive shock. Three things at once: infection + organ dysfunction + body system malfunction.
