Before you start — the big picture

Chapter 23 covers problems of the upper airway (nose, throat, larynx). The non-negotiable theme: keep the airway OPEN.

#1 priority = a patent (open) airway. Upper airway obstruction can cause respiratory arrest fast.
Epistaxis = nosebleed. OSA = obstructive sleep apnea (breathing pauses ≥10 sec during sleep). Polysomnography = the overnight sleep study that diagnoses OSA.
Watch for upper-airway obstruction signs: stridor (high-pitched crowing), restlessness, sternal retractions, hypoxia.
Head/neck cancer warning signs: hoarseness, voice change, persistent sore throat, trouble swallowing.
