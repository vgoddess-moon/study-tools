Part 1 · The Electrical Path

How the heart fires

The signal always travels the same route, top to bottom. Tap each step — notice which wave it creates on the EKG.

1
SA node fires P wave
The SA node (your natural pacemaker, top-right) sparks. The signal spreads across both atria and they squeeze. That spread = the small P wave bump.
2
AV node pauses PR interval
The signal hits the AV node and pauses for a split second — this lets the atria finish emptying blood into the ventricles. That brief flat line is the PR interval.
3
Ventricles fire QRS complex
Signal shoots down the bundle branches into the ventricles — the big, strong chambers. They squeeze hard to pump blood out. That's the tall, sharp QRS spike.
4
Ventricles reset T wave
The ventricles recharge and get ready for the next beat. This recovery shows up as the rounded T wave. Then it all repeats.

Part 3 · The Only 3 Questions

How to read any strip

You don't memorize rhythms. You ask these 3 questions in order. The answers tell you which rhythm it is.

1. Is there a P wave before every QRS?
P wave = the SA node (your normal pacemaker) is in charge. No P waves? Something else is driving the heart (atria fluttering, or the ventricles taking over).
2. Is the rhythm regular?
Are the R spikes evenly spaced, like a metronome? Irregular & no P waves → think A-fib. Regular → sinus rhythms or organized problems.
3. Is the QRS narrow or wide?
Narrow QRS = signal took the normal fast path (started above the ventricles). Wide, bizarre QRS = the ventricles are firing on their own — usually dangerous.
