The Final = Every Unit Exam You Already Took

What this is

  • The Med-Surg final retake is a paper exam the professor writes, not a HESI. It covers Chapters 1–65: Med-Surg 1 and Med-Surg 2. It is built like a regular unit exam, just longer, so this page is split the same way you learned it: by unit exam.
  • Source: the Ignatavicius 11e textbook and its Study Guide (the professor teaches from the guide), with every keyed answer checked against the guide’s answer key. Questions are in our own words.
  • Weighted toward what was actually tested: the lines the professor left as open questions on her recap sheets, the boxes and tables she said to highlight or memorize, and the topics she posted review videos on (insulin peaks, Heberden vs Bouchard nodes, Phalen’s test, traction, pendulum exercises, thyroid opposites).
  • Every chapter has at least 5 questions, so no block can bury you.

Do not skip Renal (11 AKI questions)

  • Ch 57–60 were lectured the same night as Exam 5 and were never on a unit exam. Most people skipped them. That is exactly why they are a gift on a cumulative final.
  • AKI: know the three causes (prerenal, intrarenal, postrenal), the phases (oliguric: high potassium and fluid overload; diuretic: low potassium and dehydration), and to report urine output under 30 mL/hr.

How to use it before Monday

  • Two blocks a day. Do the Med-Surg 1 blocks first if those feel far away, then Med-Surg 2, then Renal.
  • Paper Final = 60 questions drawn fresh each time from every block, in chapter order like a real cumulative exam. Take one timed (about 75 minutes) once you have done every block, and again the day before.
  • Read the hint box even when you get it right: Core fact, Cue words, Test hint. The cue words are how the question tells you which disease it means.
  • Red rows on the score page are the blocks to redo. Aim for 80% or better on the Paper Final.
