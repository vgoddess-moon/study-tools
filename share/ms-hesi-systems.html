Cue-Word Decoder — The Words That Name the Problem

How this was built

  • Source: the Ignatavicius 11e Study Guide (all 65 chapters). Every keyed answer follows its answer key; questions are rewritten in HESI style.
  • Weights: two real Med-Surg HESI reports had the same counts per topic. Teaching is the biggest single topic, then GI, Immune/Heme, Respiratory and Renal. Fundamentals (basic skills, med administration, math) is about 12 questions.
  • Fundamentals & Math and Teaching Checks were added 11/19: teaching and “which action first” questions are a big part of the real test.
  • AKI: 11 questions, on purpose. Expect it to be tested heavily.
  • Every question ends with three hints: the core fact, the cue words that name the problem, and a test-taking hint.

How to use it

  • One or two buttons a day from 11/19 to 11/23. Start with whichever topics you feel weakest in, then Fundamentals & Math and Teaching Checks.
  • Comp = 55 questions in the HESI’s proportions and the real test’s order: fluids/fundamentals first, body systems A–Z, a mixed medication block, then teaching last. A new draw every time. Take one about 11/22, timed, about 75 minutes. Aim for 38+ out of 55.
  • Read the cue words even when you get it right. Those phrases are how the HESI tells you which disease it is asking about.
  • Red rows on the score page = the systems to drill next.

🧭 Priority & First Action (66 questions)

  • The HESI separates scores on judgment: first action, priority, unexpected finding, needs more teaching, delegation. This button pulls every one of those into one drill.
  • Before you tap an answer, say three things: (1) the cue in the stem, (2) the rule that decides it (ABCs, safety, unexpected over expected, act vs. assess, infection, delegation), (3) why the runner-up loses right now.
  • After you answer, read the explanation for the option you almost picked. Each of these questions ends with a green Priority rule line.
  • Slow is the point: 20 done this way beat 55 clicked through.
